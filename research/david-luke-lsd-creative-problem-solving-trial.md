# David Luke's LSD Creative Problem-Solving Trial with PhD Scientists

**Research note compiled from public sources (October 2026)**

## Overview

David Luke (Professor of Exceptional Experience, University of Greenwich; formerly Honorary Senior Lecturer at Imperial College London) was involved in a clinical drug trial in a hospital clinical trials unit in North London, run roughly eight years before 2024 (so approximately 2018). The trial was nominally a safety and tolerability study of LSD, but Luke and colleagues embedded a creative problem-solving component by recruiting PhD scientists and asking each to bring their most intractable unsolved theoretical or technical problem.

Luke has described the trial in several public talks, including a 2024 interview ("LSD80") and a 2022 podcast (PT353, with James Fadiman and Sam Gandy). He has repeatedly stated that the study has **not been fully published** — only the pharmacokinetics have come out; the breakthrough interviews and paper-and-pencil creativity data are still forthcoming. All details below come from Luke's own public descriptions and should be treated as preliminary, not peer-reviewed evidence.

## Design

- **Setting**: Hospital clinical trials unit, North London.
- **Duration**: Luke spent about six months "babysitting" participants through their first trips.
- **Participants**: Scientists with PhDs in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM), recruited from Imperial College London, Cambridge, and Oxford. The vast majority had never taken a psychedelic before (Luke has also said two-thirds were psychedelic-naive and one-third had prior use).
- **Recruitment**: Posters in university departments (e.g., Cambridge Plant Sciences canteen) reading "Do you have a meaningful problem that needs solving? Call this number."
- **Task**: Each participant brought their most difficult theoretical or technical problem with no known solution and worked on it during the session.
- **Dose**: Around 75 micrograms — what Luke called the "Goldilocks dose" (not officially confirmed on record). A separate precognition sub-study used 50, 75, or 100 micrograms.
- **Assessment**: Interviews immediately after the trip; some psychometric and creativity tests were also administered.

## Reported Results

- Luke's characterization: **all participants had some kind of breakthrough**, small or large, some quite large.
- Cognitive pattern: increased **divergent thinking** (generating novel ideas) alongside decreased **convergent, linear, logical thinking** — most could not tie their shoelaces or operate laptops at peak, but reported having lots of good ideas.
- Luke notes these were "real-world tests," not just paper-and-pencil measures.

## Notable Breakthrough Examples

1. **Intelligent fabric / chain mail scientist** (materials science PhD): Had been working on a programmable chain-mail fabric that could be shaped into any 3D laminate form; it was not working. Within half an hour of coming up on LSD he visualized the solution, sketched it down, later 3D-printed it, patented it, and Luke says it is now being developed by NASA and MIT.

2. **Tectonic plates researcher**: Had three years of data on plate evolution over millions of years that made no coherent sense. On LSD he experienced diving to the ocean floor and watching the plates evolve in fast-forward according to his data; he realized some of his data was wrong and gained a coherent theory.

3. **Merlin Sheldrake** (biologist, Cambridge PhD): Studying the relationship between the non-photosynthetic "ghost plant" *Voyria* and its fungal partner in Panama, long assumed to be purely parasitic. On LSD he described "turning into the fungus" — finding himself underground among surging hyphal tips, following a fungal hypha into a cavernous root and experiencing it as a sanctuary. This shifted his framing from parasitism to mutual aid and reciprocity. He later wrote that LSD "forced me to admit I had an imagination, and I now saw fungi differently." He made no claims about the factual validity of the visions — "at best plausible, and at worst delirious nonsense. Not even wrong." The experience fed into his book *Entangled Life* (2020), which won the Royal Society Science Book Prize.

4. **Quantum physicist** (working on the Higgs boson at CERN): Reported excitement about "seeing the gravitational waves," previously only a mathematical construct.

## The Embedded Precognition Experiment

Luke also embedded a precognition task in the same trial (he has called this "sneaking" it into the protocol). Details from his public talks:

- **Participants**: 13 scientists from the same trial.
- **Design**: Double-blind; participants did not know whether they were on LSD. Each did two trials — one on LSD, one not — using a true random number generator. The task involved visualizing/viewing and ranking target material (Luke has mentioned fractal-image or clip-ranking formats in his other precognition work).
- **Doses**: 50, 75, or 100 micrograms.
- **Baseline**: Pre-LSD scores were pretty much at chance.
- **LSD condition**: Significant scoring — but in the **opposite direction** from expectation (a "sign-missing" effect). Participants tended to rank the actual target lowest rather than highest.
- **Luke's interpretation**: All but one participant had expressed disbelief in parapsychological phenomena. He suggests the scientists were using their psychedelic precognition to "show that precognition isn't real" by identifying the target and rating it as the absolute lowest. He also found that openness to experience and extraversion predicted performance, and that the scientists scored low on openness, were somewhat introverted, and quite neurotic.
- **Dose effect**: An inverse relationship between dose and ability to visualize the target — those on 100 micrograms did worse and reported finding it very difficult to visualize, which Luke attributed to being too high to hold anything concrete long enough to verbalize.
- **Caveats Luke himself raised**: The LSD condition was always second (order effect possible, not a true LSD effect); small sample (N=13); single trial per condition.
- **Publication status**: Reported in conference presentations (e.g., "Psychedelomancy: controlled experiments in psychedelic precognition," Breaking Convention 2017) but not in a peer-reviewed journal. Luke has said the results are forthcoming.

## Context: The 1966 Harman et al. Study This Trial Replicated

Luke's trial was explicitly a conceptual replication of the 1966 study by Willis Harman, Robert McKim, Robert Mogar, James Fadiman, and Myron Stolaroff at the International Foundation for Advanced Study in Menlo Park, California (*Psychological Reports*, 19, 211–227).

- **Participants**: 27 professionally employed males (16 engineers, 1 engineer-physicist, 2 mathematicians, 2 architects, 1 psychologist, 1 furniture designer, 1 commercial artist, 1 sales manager, 1 personnel manager), in 7 small groups of 3–4.
- **Drugs**: LSD-25 (50 micrograms for one group of research engineers; the other group received 100 mg mescaline).
- **Design**: Single psychedelic session following extensive selection and preparatory procedures, with careful attention to expectancies and a psychosocial milieu conducive to creative activity. Participants worked on real professional problems in which they had a personal stake.
- **Findings**: All participants felt the drug had subjectively enhanced their creative process. Eleven core themes of "strategies of enhanced functioning" were identified: low inhibition and anxiety; capacity to restructure the problem in a larger context; enhanced fluency and flexibility of ideation; heightened capacity for visual imagery and fantasy; increased ability to concentrate; heightened empathy with external processes and objects; heightened empathy with people; subconscious data more accessible; association of dissimilar ideas; heightened motivation to obtain closure; and visualizing the completed solution. Solutions obtained included a new approach to a vibratory microtome design, a commercial building design accepted by the client, space probe experiments, a linear electron accelerator beam-steering device, an engineering improvement to a magnetic tape recorder, a chair design modeled and accepted by the manufacturer, and a letterhead design approved by the customer. Enhanced creative ability appeared to persist for at least some weeks.
- **Limitation**: No control group; effects could not be separated from expectation and the structured setting.

## Important Caveats

1. The creative problem-solving trial has **not been peer-reviewed or fully published**. All breakthrough claims are Luke's own characterization from talks and interviews.
2. There is **no control group**, so drug effects cannot be separated from expectation, selection (highly motivated scientists with personal stakes), and the clinical setting.
3. The precognition sub-study's "sign-missing" result is statistically interesting but rests on N=13, a fixed order (LSD always second), and Luke's post-hoc personality interpretation.
4. The 1966 study is a pilot with the same core limitation (no controls) and a small, non-random sample.
5. Where Luke has given numbers (e.g., "all had breakthroughs," dose ranges), they come from his oral accounts and may shift as formal publication approaches.
