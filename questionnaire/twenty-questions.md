# The Twenty Questions

Designed as a funnel: start with the everyday, descend into the broken places, and let the listener finish the descent themselves.

## Round One — The Everyday Nudge

1. Have you ever felt a sudden urge to do something — go into a shop, take a different road — with no obvious reason?
2. Did you follow it or ignore it?
3. What happened?
4. If you ignored it, do you ever wonder what you missed?
5. If you followed it, did it change anything?

## Round Two — The Vision

6. When you picture a future you want — a house, a job, a holiday — where does that picture come from?
7. Can you see it clearly, like a film?
8. Have you ever pictured something and then made it happen?
9. What was the first step between the picture and the reality?
10. Did the picture feel like it came from you, or through you?

## Round Three — The Broken Places

11. Have you ever been so low that nothing outside helped — not family, not friends?
12. What was that like?
13. In that silence, did a voice speak?
14. What did it say?
15. Did it feel like your own thoughts, or something else?

## Round Four — The Fork

16. When you say you're not spiritual, what do you mean?
17. Is it God you don't believe in, or the inner life?
18. If the voice that said "you're going to be okay" was real — what was it?
19. Where do you think thoughts come from?
20. If you could ask one question about all of this, what would it be?

## Host Notes

- Open with your own story — the broken places, the voice. Ten minutes, no questions. That earns the room's trust.
- Frame it as "help me understand what you mean" — genuine curiosity lands, interrogation doesn't.
- Record it. The raw audio of people fumbling for an answer to "who spoke?" is worth more than any polished conclusion.
- The last question hands the room the microphone and lets them finish the descent themselves.
