# Purpose: Funding Model for Building Acquisition

**Status:** Draft — compiled from research, October 2026
**Context:** Entheogenic religion project (ConsciousMonkeys-dot/entheogenic-religion)

---

## The Goal

Acquire a building for the religion — a permanent home for worship, community, and ritual. The model below is drawn from how mosques in Northern Ireland actually raised their money, and from how the two largest ayahuasca churches fund and hold property, not from theory.

## The Model (from real NI case studies)

### 1. Belfast Islamic Centre (BIC) — Aldersgate House, University Road
- Raised **£800,000** to buy the building for £750,000 (2015/2016).
- Funding mix: locally generated funds from the community + generous overseas funders, particularly **Oman**.
- The overseas source was named publicly by the centre itself, but no donor name, amount, or channel was ever disclosed. The country is known; the money isn't.
- Later refurbishment (£1.676 million): £500,000 grant from the Department for Communities for the *cultural* elements only (fitness classes, food bank, youth club, language classes) — the prayer space was funded by the community. Belfast City Council managed the works.
- BIC is affiliated to the Muslim Council of Great Britain.

### 2. Iqraa Mosque — Dunmurry (former Presbyterian church)
- Bought the building for **£200,000**.
- Funded by loans from supporters, repaid £150,000, £50,000 outstanding.
- Raised a further £100,000 for renovations (wudu area, facility for washing the deceased).
- The Belfast Iqraa School (charity NIC107402, registered 2020, ~£78,000/year income) ran a JustGiving appeal (£5,342 raised), a GoFundMe (£4,300), and a live telethon on Islam TV broadcast to over a million viewers worldwide (2021).
- No government money. Community donations only.
- The church was originally marketed at £1.5 million (2008), re-listed at £300,000 (2016) — the mosque group bought at a steep discount from the original asking price.

### 3. The NI pattern
- **No single cheque.** The £800,000 was years of small donations from a community of 40+ nationalities, plus one overseas source.
- **Overseas money is normal**, not unusual — transnational giving is standard in this community.
- **Public money funds the shell, not the soul.** DfC paid for cultural elements; the prayer space was community-funded. Heritage and community-infrastructure grants exist for buildings that serve the wider public.
- **Buildings are cheap when congregations die.** Dunmurry Presbyterian sold for a fraction of its peak value. Empty churches and office blocks are the realistic acquisition targets.

## The Closer Model: UDV and Santo Daime

These are the religions that already solved the building problem for a plant-sacrament faith. Their money is member money. Neither relies on a named foreign state.

### União do Vegetal (Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal)
- Founded 22 July 1961 by José Gabriel da Costa (Mestre Gabriel). General headquarters moved to Brasília in 1982.
- Scale (official figures): about **226 núcleos and 21 distributions**, in every Brazilian state plus the United States, Canada, the UK, Spain, Portugal, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Australia, Italy and Peru. Over **24,000 members** and more than 6,000 children of members.
- **Funding rule, stated by the General Directorate:** all officers work voluntarily. The UDV receives **no money from private companies or the public sector**. Maintenance comes from **monthly membership payments and donations from members**.
- Institutional film confirms the same: every member pays a fixed monthly amount to keep the núcleo running. No mestre, counsellor or pastor is paid.
- Brazil granted federal public-utility status in 1999. Some social work is done in partnership with public bodies. That is separate from the religious budget.
- **Property doctrine (US litigation):** each church is expected to work toward **owning the land and the building** where it holds services. In Santa Fe, a member who owned the land signed a purchase agreement to transfer it to the church for a nominal sum, on condition it stayed in religious use.
- **Land-use fight, not a funding fight:** Santa Fe County refused a temple permit in 2011. The US branch sued under the First Amendment, RLUIPA and the New Mexico Religious Freedom Restoration Act. Settlement in 2012 let the temple go ahead, with conditions (services inside, a sound wall, county water and sewage). Licence to build issued December 2014. The sacrament case itself was *Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal* (US Supreme Court, unanimous, 2006).
- US branch incorporated in New Mexico in 1993. Jeffrey Bronfman was among the founders — a reminder that early capital can come from a wealthy member, not a foreign treasury.

### Santo Daime / ICEFLU (Igreja do Culto Eclético da Fluente Luz Universal)
- Patron Sebastião Mota de Melo. Spiritual headquarters at Vila Céu do Mapiá, inside Floresta Nacional do Purus, Amazonas. More than 100 churches treat the Cathedral of the Queen of the Forest as the mother church.
- **Principal revenue is membership dues**, chosen deliberately over visitor donations. Current bands, as a share of the national minimum wage: carente 2% (north only), social 5%, semi-plena 10%, intermediate 15%, plena 20%, special above 20%. Under-18s and over-65s are exempt.
- Local groups must set a local monthly fee from the start (even as a Ponto) sized to their own costs. Self-sufficiency in sacrament supply — plantings on land the church owns — is required once a group reaches Centro status.
- **Property rule, written into institutional guidance:** the land under the temple, the children's house, the feitio house, and the jagube and rainha plantings **must be in the name of the local church or of ICEFLU**, by deed or by a private cession of use.
- Other income: donations from members and sympathisers, and social/environmental projects run through partnerships, contracts and public calls (editais), often via Instituto CEFLURIS and AMAGAIA. That is project money, not the worship budget.
- **Capital campaign, not a state grant:** the Cathedral (about 5,700 m², capacity around 2,000) was built over many years with community labour plus a contracted engineer (Engecampo). The remaining floor (2,000 m²) is an open international appeal — goal about R$2 million / US$343,000 / €301,000 — aimed at the brotherhood in Brazil and abroad. Materials for earlier stages were barged in (137 tonnes reported in one bulletin).

### What this changes for the NI plan
- The mosque path (unnamed overseas donor + cultural grant) is available, but it is the wrong fit if the sacrament is the point. Public funders will pay for a hall, a food project, a heritage shell. They will not pay for the ritual.
- The UDV/ICEFLU path is: **monthly dues first, property in the church's name, capital appeals for the building, voluntary leadership, sacrament grown or held on church land where the law allows.** Visitor donations exist; they are not the base.
- The hard problem in the US was permission to build and permission to use the sacrament, not the money. Money followed membership. In Northern Ireland the same split applies: charity registration and Gift Aid solve the money mechanics; planning and the legal status of any sacrament are a separate fight.

## The Replicable Path for NI

1. **Register as a charity** with the Charity Commission NI. This unlocks **Gift Aid** — adds 25% to every UK donation at no cost to the donor.
2. **Set a membership due** before chasing a building. UDV and ICEFLU both treat the monthly payment as the institution, not an optional extra. Tier it. Exempt children and pensioners.
3. **Run parallel fundraising channels for capital only:** crowdfunding, direct bank transfers, a livestream appeal, and personal loans from supporters repaid over time. Keep operating costs on dues.
4. **Put the building in the organisation's name**, or secure a written cession of use. ICEFLU requires this for temple, school and plantings. A member holding the deeds is a future dispute.
5. **Target buildings that can also serve the wider community** if public money is wanted — halls, education, heritage. That is the BIC £500k precedent and the Community Infrastructure Fund (~£4 million, includes church halls). Do not expect that money to cover ritual.
6. **Watch the market for distressed religious property** — churches closing because attendance fell sell at deep discounts. Treasure Ireland (£423k to 80 churches, 2020–2026) shows how much state money already goes into keeping religious buildings standing.

## Open Questions

- Who exactly in Oman funded BIC, and how much? The accounts don't say. England & Wales annual returns now ask charities to declare foreign income by country; NI's Charity Commission has no equivalent question.
- BIC's latest accounts are 239 days overdue on the register.
- What is the actual split between local and overseas donations for any of the NI mosque projects? Nobody outside the trustees knows.
- UDV and ICEFLU publish the rule (dues, no corporate or state money for the centre; property in the church's name) but not a public ledger of what each núcleo costs to build.
