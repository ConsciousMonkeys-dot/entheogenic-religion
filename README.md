# Entheogenic Religion

A project to develop an entheogenic religion — a faith where plant medicine is the sacrament itself, not an accessory.

## The Word

**Entheogenic** — from the Greek *entheos* (full of the god) and *genesthai* (to generate). Coined in 1979 by Carl Ruck, Jeremy Bigwood, Danny Staples, Richard Evans Schultes, and Jonathan Ott, to replace "hallucinogen" — which framed the experience as delusion rather than revelation.

An entheogenic religion is one where the plant is the doorway to the divine, not a shortcut around it.

## The Lineage

- **UDV (União do Vegetal)** — Brazilian church founded 1961 by Mestre Gabriel. Ayahuasca (hoasca) as sacrament. Won US Supreme Court case Gonzales v. UDV (2006) for religious exemption.
- **Santo Daime** — Brazilian syncretic religion from the 1930s, Christian and Indigenous roots, hymns and ritual discipline.
- **Rastafari** — ganja as sacrament, drawing on Genesis 1:29, Psalm 104:14, Revelation 22:2. Legal in Jamaica since 2015.
- **Hindu / Sufi traditions** — bhang as prasad of Shiva, the Atharvaveda names bhanga among five sacred plants. Sufi poets wrote of hashish and the mystery of "I am He."
- **Ancient Israel** — the *keneh bosem* (fragrant cane) in the holy anointing oil of Exodus 30; cannabis residue found on the Tel Arad altar (c. 760–715 BCE), the first known evidence of a hallucinogenic substance in the Kingdom of Judah.

## The Core Idea

The same faculty that pictures a renovated kitchen or a new job is the faculty that receives the vision. The question is not whether God exists — it is whether you have noticed you are already using it.

The inner voice that says "you're going to be okay" when nothing outside is helping — that is the thing the religion is built around.

## The Two Truths

Every tradition points at the same source through different doors. The religion does not flatten the traditions into "shared values" — it honours the edges while recognising the common root.
