# The Daily Tea Liturgy

## Purpose

The herbal medicine menu tells us *what* to drink. This liturgy tells us *how* to drink it — turning a remedy into a sacrament. The body is the vessel of consciousness; a clear body supports a clear mind, and a clear mind is the doorway to gnosis. Each cup is a small act of devotion, not just a treatment.

This liturgy is the companion to [The Herbal Medicine Menu](herbal-medicine-menu.md), which lists every herb, its uses, the illness categories, and the weekly routine.

## The Liturgy

### 1. Intention (before the first sip)

Hold the cup with both hands. Breathe once, slowly. Say:

> "I drink this as medicine, not as escape."

That single sentence is the whole theology in miniature. The plant is a gift of creation — the same creation that gave frankincense and myrrh at the nativity. We receive it with gratitude, not craving.

### 2. Preparation (the act of making)

The making is itself prayer. Crushing the resin, slicing the ginger, steeping the herbs — these are slow, deliberate movements. Do not rush them. The time spent preparing is time spent in the body, present, rather than in the mind, elsewhere.

If you are making a resin tea (frankincense or myrrh), remember: these are the founding sacraments of the tradition. The Magi brought them to the child. You are continuing that offering, in your own kitchen, in your own cup.

### 3. Drinking (the act of receiving)

Sip slowly. Notice the taste — bitter, warm, resinous, sweet. Do not mask it with sugar. The bitterness is part of the medicine; it teaches the body to receive what it needs rather than what it wants.

Between sips, breathe. Let the warmth settle. If a thought arises — worry, distraction, restlessness — return to the cup. The tea is the anchor.

### 4. Closing (after the last sip)

Set the cup down. Place a hand over the heart or the belly. Say:

> "The vessel is tended. The mind is clear. I am ready."

Then go about your day. The liturgy is complete in the time it takes to drink one cup.

## The Weekly Rhythm

Follow the weekly routine in the [herbal medicine menu](herbal-medicine-menu.md). The liturgy does not change day to day — only the herbs change. Monday's ginger-turmeric and Sunday's spruce needle are the same sacrament in different forms.

## The Seasonal Shift

In winter, lean toward spruce needle, elderberry, and warming roots. In summer, lean toward hibiscus, rooibos, and cooling herbs. The liturgy stays the same; the medicine follows the season, as creation itself does.

## Notes for the Community

- This liturgy is for personal daily practice. It does not replace medical care.
- The safety checklist in the [herbal medicine menu](herbal-medicine-menu.md) still applies.
- If you share this practice with others, the intention line is the one thing to pass on first. Everything else can be learned; that sentence is the seed.
