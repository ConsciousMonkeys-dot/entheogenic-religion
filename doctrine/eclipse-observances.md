# Eclipse Observances

## Purpose

An eclipse is not an ordinary moon. It is a moon that meets the sun's path — the nodes, where the moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic. When the alignment is close enough, one body swallows the other, and the sky goes dark in the middle of the day, or the full moon turns red.

The tradition reads this as a decision point. The sun is will; the moon is intuition. When one swallows the other, something is being decided — and the community marks it.

This document sets out how eclipses are observed. It is the companion to the [celestial calendar](celestial-calendar.md), which holds the monthly and yearly rhythms. Eclipses sit above both: rarer, heavier, and treated as their own category.

## The Two Kinds

**Solar eclipse — a new moon that swallows the sun.**

The dark of the month arrives with extra weight. The intention set on this day carries further than an ordinary new moon — but so does anything begun carelessly. The old teaching: do not start what you are not ready to finish. A solar eclipse is a threshold. Cross it with intention or not at all.

**Lunar eclipse — a full moon that turns red.**

The peak of the month arrives shadowed. What was harvested is now tested. The red moon is the tradition's image of release under pressure: what survives the shadow was real; what dissolves was ready to go. A lunar eclipse is a completion — the end of a cycle that has been building for weeks.

## 2027 Eclipses

These dates are calculated from the moon's cycle and the position of the lunar nodes. They should be verified against an ephemeris before any observance is fixed — the node positions drift, and a near-miss can become a total eclipse or nothing at all.

- **May 19–20 — Lunar eclipse (full moon in Taurus).** The year's first eclipse. A completion in the sign of stability and the body. The tradition reads this as the harvest of everything planted since the spring equinox being weighed.
- **June 2–3 — Solar eclipse (new moon in Gemini).** Opens on the same days as the June Mercury retrograde. Inward energy, review energy, and a swallowed sun arriving together — the heaviest inward month of the year. Deep work only. No new beginnings.

No other eclipses fall in 2027. The next pair comes in 2028.

## The Observance

Eclipses are marked differently from ordinary moons:

1. **Silence before.** From the day before the eclipse until the day after, the community keeps a lighter schedule — fewer words, fewer commitments, no major decisions. The threshold is crossed slowly.

2. **One act, not many.** An ordinary new moon invites many intentions. An eclipse invites one. Choose it before the eclipse begins, speak it once, and let it stand.

3. **No starting, no finishing.** Do not begin a project on an eclipse day, and do not force a conclusion. The eclipse is the pause between — the held breath.

4. **Afterward: three days of quiet.** The energy of an eclipse does not settle in one night. The three days following are for noticing what shifted, not for acting on it.

## The Safety Layer

Eclipses are astronomy, not omens of harm. Looking at a solar eclipse without proper eye protection causes permanent retinal damage — the sky does not care about intention. If a solar eclipse is visible from where you are, use certified eclipse glasses or an indirect viewing method. The tradition's first rule is that the body is the vessel: protect the vessel.

- Should the silence-before rule apply to everyone, or only to those who choose it?
- How should a partial eclipse (where the sun or moon is only partly covered) be marked compared to a total one?
