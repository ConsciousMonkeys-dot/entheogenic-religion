# The Celestial Calendar

## Purpose

The tradition does not invent its own time. It reads the time that already exists — the sky. The moon, the sun, and the planets move in rhythms older than any religion, and the faith aligns its practice to those rhythms rather than imposing a human schedule on them.

This document is the first draft of that alignment. The dates below are calculated for 2027 (tropical zodiac). They should be verified against an ephemeris before any observance is fixed — astronomy is precise; this draft is a starting point.

## The Moon: The Monthly Rhythm

The moon completes a full cycle every 29.5 days. That cycle is the backbone of the calendar.

**New moon** — the dark of the month. Energy turns inward. This is the time to rest, to set intention, to begin. Plant: begin new remedies, start a cleanse, plant seeds (literal or figurative).

**Waxing moon** — energy builds. This is the time to act, to move, to push outward. Plant: active work, building, learning, starting projects.

**Full moon** — peak energy. The month's culmination. This is the time to harvest, to celebrate, to release what is complete. Plant: harvest herbs, complete cycles, celebrate.

**Waning moon** — energy recedes. This is the time to release, to let go, to go inward. Plant: release old habits, end cycles, rest.

## The Sun: The Yearly Rhythm

The sun moves through the twelve signs of the zodiac, one per month, completing the circle each year. Each sign carries its own quality, and the tradition reads that quality as a season of the soul.

### The Four Gates (Solstices and Equinoxes)

The four points where the sun crosses the equator or reaches its extremes are the year's major observances:

- **Spring Equinox — March 20.** Light returns. The year begins. New beginnings, planting, the intention of the whole year is set here.
- **Summer Solstice — June 21.** Peak light. Celebration, harvest of the first growth, the outward year at its height.
- **Autumn Equinox — September 23.** Balance of light and dark. Harvest, gratitude, release of what the year produced.
- **Winter Solstice — December 22.** Longest night. The inward year begins. Rest, reflection, the seed of what comes next.

## The Planets: The Daily and Weekly Rhythm

Each planet carries a quality. The tradition assigns each day to a planet, following the ancient order (the days of the week themselves are named for them):

| Day | Planet | Quality | Practice |
|-----|--------|---------|----------|
| Sunday | Sun | Vitality, will | Active, outward, leadership |
| Monday | Moon | Emotion, intuition | Rest, dream, go inward |
| Tuesday | Mars | Action, courage | Physical work, movement, resolve |
| Wednesday | Mercury | Mind, communication | Study, speak, write, travel |
| Thursday | Jupiter | Expansion, wisdom | Teach, learn, give, celebrate |
| Friday | Venus | Love, beauty, harmony | Create, relate, enjoy |
| Saturday | Saturn | Structure, discipline, rest | Rest, reflect, complete, release |

### Mercury Retrograde

Three times a year, Mercury appears to move backward through the sky. In the tradition this is not a curse — it is a review. The old teaching is: do not start new things during retrograde; review, revise, and repair what already exists.

**2027 retrograde windows (approximate — verify before fixing dates):**

- February 10 – March 5
- June 3 – June 27
- October 7 – October 28

Note that the June window opens on a new moon (June 2–3), which the tradition reads as a double signal: inward energy and review energy arriving together. That is a month for deep inner work, not outward action.

## How the Layers Stack

A day is read by stacking all three layers:

1. **The moon** — where in its 29.5-day cycle. High energy (full) or low energy (new).
2. **The sun** — which sign it occupies. The season of the soul.
3. **The day** — which planet rules it. The daily quality.

Example: a full moon on a Thursday in Sagittarius is a peak-energy day ruled by Jupiter — a day for celebration, teaching, and outward expansion. A new moon on a Saturday in Pisces is a deep-rest day ruled by Saturn — a day for silence, release, and going inward.

## The Practice

- **New moon:** set the intention for the coming cycle. Begin what you want to grow.
- **Full moon:** harvest, celebrate, release what is complete.
- **Solstices and equinoxes:** the four major observances of the year. Community gatherings where possible.
- **Mercury retrograde:** review and repair. No new beginnings.
- **Daily:** let the planet of the day shape the quality of the work.

## Open Questions

- Should the year begin at the spring equinox, the winter solstice, or the first new moon after one of them?
- Should the tradition use the tropical zodiac (as above) or the sidereal zodiac used in Vedic and some other traditions?
- How should lunar eclipses and solar eclipses be marked — as intensified versions of full and new moons, or as separate observances?
- Should the community adopt a shared calendar with fixed feast days, or should each person read their own sky?
