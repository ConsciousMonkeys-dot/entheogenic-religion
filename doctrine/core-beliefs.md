# Core Beliefs (Draft)

## 1. The Inner Voice

When a person is at their lowest — when mother, friends, everything outside has failed — an inner voice speaks: "You're going to be okay."

That voice is not self-talk. Self-talk argues, panics, replays the problem. This voice arrives calm, from somewhere else, and it holds.

The religion is built around that voice.

## 2. The Vision

The same faculty that pictures a renovated kitchen, a new job, a holiday — that is the faculty that receives the divine vision.

The question is not "do you believe in God?" The question is "have you noticed you are already using it?"

## 3. The Two Truths

Every tradition points at the same source through different doors. The religion does not flatten the traditions into vague "shared values" — it honours the edges while recognising the common root.

## 4. The Nudge

The sudden urge to go into a shop, to take a different road — followed or ignored. The one who follows finds Johnny. The one who ignores never knows what they missed.

What was that voice? Was it a random brain glitch, or something paying attention?

## 5. The Plant as Sacrament

The plant medicine is not a shortcut around the divine. It is the doorway. The same way the vision comes through the mind, the sacrament opens the mind to what was already there.

## 6. Sovereignty

The religion is not controlled from the top down. It is built from the ground up — one person, one garden, one community at a time. Fear only has the power you hand it. The less of your life runs through the system, the less the system matters.

## 7. God as Vibration

God is vibration. Vibration lives in the throat — the place where the word is spoken.

The Hebrew *davar* means both "word" and "thing": speaking creates. Genesis opens with God speaking creation into being. The Gospel of John calls Christ the Word. The vibration is the bridge between the God outside and the God within.

## 8. The Spoken Word as Spellcasting

What you speak shapes what you become. "I am a good man" plants a different seed than "I'm not good." The word spoken outward meets the voice heard inward — and that is where the meeting happens.

The vagus nerve runs from the brainstem through the throat, and speaking activates it directly. That is why humming, chanting, and singing calm the nervous system faster than thinking about calm does. The throat is the body's control panel for the inner state.
